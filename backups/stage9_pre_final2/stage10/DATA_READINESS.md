# SAGE Enterprise Data Readiness Specification

This document outlines the data requirements, prerequisites, operational constraints, and methodological boundaries for deploying SAGE on real company enterprise systems (ERP, MES, WMS).

---

## 1. Required Data Schema & Columns

To construct the daily operational panel for causal evaluation, the enterprise must supply daily operational data or an automated export that maps to the following core entities and dimensions:

### A. Calendar & Temporal Features
- `date` (**Mandatory**): ISO 8601 format (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Continuous daily records without gaps.
- `weekday`, `month`, `is_weekend` (Auto-generated by SAGE if `date` is present).

### B. Procurement & Supplier Performance (Per Supplier / Vendor ID)
- `supplier_<ID>_po_delay_days`: Days overdue for currently active open purchase orders relative to promised delivery date (0.0 if on-time or closed).
- `supplier_<ID>_receipt_fill_rate`: Ratio of quantity received on receipt date versus outstanding ordered quantity (1.0 on normal receipts).
- `supplier_<ID>_receipt_event_flag`: Binary indicator (1 if a goods receipt occurred today, 0 otherwise).

### C. Inventory & Materials Management (Per Raw Material SKU)
- `material_<ID>_stock`: End-of-day stock on hand (units/kg/liters).
- `material_<ID>_days_of_cover`: Current stock divided by the trailing 28-day average planned consumption.
- `material_<ID>_open_po_flag`: Binary indicator (1 if there is an unfulfilled purchase order active, 0 otherwise).
- `material_<ID>_reorder_triggered`: Binary indicator (1 if a replenishment order was generated today).

### D. Manufacturing & Assembly (Per Finished Good SKU)
- `product_<ID>_fg_stock`: End-of-day finished goods inventory on hand.
- `product_<ID>_planned_production`: Work orders planned for execution today.
- `product_<ID>_actual_production`: Good units completed and booked off work orders today.
- `product_<ID>_production_shortfall`: Difference `max(0, planned - actual)`.
- `product_<ID>_capacity_shortfall_units`: Production loss attributable to workcenter bottlenecks/downtime rather than raw material stockouts.

### E. Demand & Fulfillment (Per Finished Good SKU)
- `product_<ID>_demand_units`: New customer order quantity booked today.
- `product_<ID>_due_backlog_units`: Cumulative unfulfilled customer order units whose requested delivery date has passed.
- `product_<ID>_newly_backordered_units`: Customer order units whose delivery date arrived today but could not be fulfilled.
- `product_<ID>_backorder_flag`: Binary indicator (1 if cumulative backlog > 0).

---

## 2. Minimum Data History Requirement

- **Minimum Time Horizon**: **At least 1 continuous year (365 daily rows)** of operational data.
- **Why 1 year is non-negotiable**:
  1. **Calendar & Seasonality Conditioning**: Causal attribution algorithms require observing the full cycle of weekdays, weekends, end-of-month surges, and annual holiday shutdowns to distinguish genuine supply-chain disruptions from predictable demand rhythm.
  2. **Null Threshold Calibration**: SAGE builds empirical null baseline distributions using bootstrap windows from stable periods. At least 250–300 days of normal operations are necessary to fit credible 95th percentile confidence envelopes.
  3. **Lag Dependencies**: Stock-to-stock and PO-lead-time feedback loops operate across multi-week horizons.

---

## 3. What SAGE Cannot Do on Real Company Data

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Fundamental Methodological Boundary:**
> *"SAGE performs controlled synthetic causal-attribution evaluation under an expert-defined causal model; it does not claim to prove real-world causality."*

When applied to real company enterprise data, plant managers and executives must understand the following limitations:

1. **No Real-World Ground Truth Answer Key**:
   - In synthetic simulations, the exact injected disturbance is known with mathematical certainty. In real enterprises, no system possesses a "divine label" proving that late delivery of Steel Lot #402 caused a 3-day assembly line stoppage versus an undocumented equipment micro-fault.
   - SAGE cannot "prove" physical causality; it measures statistical consistency with an explicit structural hypothesis.

2. **Mandatory Expert DAG Review by Plant Personnel**:
   - Causal graphical models (DAGs) cannot be reliably discovered purely from observational data without strong unprovable assumptions (faithfulness, acyclicity, causal sufficiency).
   - Before SAGE can evaluate a new facility, the plant manager, chief metallurgist, and logistics planner **must inspect and approve the causal DAG**. The arrows reflect expert domain knowledge of physical factory flow; SAGE does not invent or discover this graph out of thin air.

3. **No Unobserved Confounder Protection**:
   - If an unrecorded factor affects both supplier delivery and plant throughput (e.g., an unlogged regional blizzard, a localized power grid brownout, or an undocumented ERP clerical error), SAGE cannot mathematically detect it unless the factor is measured in the input dataset.

4. **Prescriptive Action Boundaries**:
   - SAGE identifies root-cause attribution candidates and orders them by evidence strength ("Strong evidence", "Some evidence", "Weak evidence", "Nothing unusual").
   - SAGE does **not** replace plant management discretion. Operational remediation (e.g., expediting freight, retooling workcenters) remains the exclusive prerogative of human staff.
